Your first five minutes

1Signing in

  1. Open https://sheontherun.com/admin and add it to your phone's home screen, so it's one tap next time.
  2. Type your email, then your password.
  3. Open your authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy or 1Password) and type the 6-digit code it shows for SheOnTheRun.
  4. If you ever lose your phone, choose "I can't use my app — use a recovery code" and use one of the codes you saved. Each one works once.
Screenshot to add: the sign-in screen
Keep your recovery codes safe. They are the only way in if your phone is lost. Save them in your password manager or printed somewhere private — not in your email.

Creating your owner account (the first time)

You do this once, and it makes you the website's first administrator. Nobody else creates an account or knows your password. You receive one private setup key through a password-manager share; it is not an invite and it stops working as soon as you finish.

  1. Open the admin address. The first-time owner setup screen appears automatically.
  2. Type your private setup key, your email, and choose your password (at least 12 characters). Type it twice.
  3. Press Continue, then set up your phone code: install an authenticator app, scan the square code on screen, and type the 6 digits it shows.
  4. Save the recovery codes it gives you, tick the box, and continue. You're in.
The private setup key works only until your account is made. The screen closes permanently after that, so you become the sole administrator.

3Save, Preview, Publish — the one idea

This is the whole trick, and it means you can never break the website by accident:

  • Save keeps your work as a draft. The website does not change yet.
  • Preview opens the real website with your drafts on top — only you can see it. A small yellow "not published" dot stays until you publish.
  • Publish puts every saved change live at once, in English and Arabic. It takes a second; visitors may need to refresh.
  • Changed your mind? Publish → Discard all unpublished changes throws the drafts away and the website stays exactly as it was.
  • Publish → History keeps every publish. Restore copies an old version back into your drafts (publish again to make it live).
  • Working on two devices? If a change was saved somewhere else, the panel tells you and asks you to reload — nothing is lost.

4Orders

  1. Open Orders; the badge in the menu shows how many are new.
  2. Open one to see the items, phone number, address and total. The website works out the total from your prices, never the customer's.
  3. Move it along: New → Confirmed → Out for delivery → Delivered, or Cancelled (cancelling puts the stock back automatically).
  4. Paid? For a Whish or OMT transfer, tick Paid and paste the reference.
  5. WhatsApp opens a chat with that customer, the order already written out for you.
  6. Export CSV downloads everything for your records.
  7. Notes are private — only admins ever see them.

5Messages

Open a message, reply from your own email, then mark it Handled. The badge in the menu shows what's new.

6Shop & products

  • Add a product with + Add: name, short description, price, sizes or options, and a photo.
  • Stock: type how many are left per size. At 0 the size (or product) shows as sold out by itself. Leave a box empty to say "not tracked".
  • Reorder with the ↑ ↓ arrows; Duplicate or Delete from inside an item.
  • Every English field has its Arabic twin beside it. A yellow "Arabic missing" flag means the Arabic side is still empty — fill it before publishing.
  • Delivery governorates offered at checkout live here too.

7Runs & events

  • Add a run, class or event: date and time (Beirut time), place, details and places left.
  • Add 8 places and the site shows "8 places left"; 0 shows "Fully booked".
  • Tick featured to push it to the top.
  • Past events disappear from the site by themselves once their end time passes — no need to delete them.

8Services & packages

Change a consultation's price or description, what a package includes, its duration, whether it's in person or online, and the BackOnTheRun challenge. The same Save → Publish rule applies.

9Testimonials

Add real client quotes and tick the "permission given" box before publishing one. (The three quotes already there are marked sample — replace them with real ones when you can.)

10Site settings

  • WhatsApp number — digits only, full international format (Lebanon starts with 961). Every "Order" and "Book" button uses it.
  • Emails — which inbox receives each kind of Connect-form message.
  • Instagram / LinkedIn — leave a field empty to hide that link.
  • Switches for taking shop orders and Connect-form messages inside this admin.
  • Address and map for the Connect page.
While the WhatsApp number still reads [[WHATSAPP NUMBER]], booking buttons fall back to the Connect page. Set the real number here and the WhatsApp buttons come alive.

11Photos & galleries

  1. Photos → Add a photo: pick the file, write a short description in English and Arabic (it's read aloud to people who can't see it), give it a name, and upload.
  2. The server makes the sizes the website needs — a photo straight from your phone is fine.
  3. The "Choose photo" pickers in products, events and articles now list it.
  4. A photo that's part of the website's own pages can be described and replaced, not deleted.
  5. Photo galleries decides which photos appear in the scrolling galleries, and in what order.

12Journal

  1. Journal → + Add article: topic, title, date, reading time, summary, cover photo, then write in the box. Headings, lists, quotes and links are in the toolbar.
  2. Tick Keep as a draft while you're working — a draft isn't on the website at all.
  3. Save, Preview to read it, then Publish. Publishing also updates the article list, the RSS feed and Google's sitemap for you.

13Meal plans

  1. Meal plans → Add a plan file: upload the file you made for a client — a plan page (.html), a spreadsheet plan (.csv) or a saved copy (.json).
  2. Make it the sample decides what the public "Try the sample plan" button opens. That choice goes live with Publish. The spreadsheet template the site hands out can't be deleted.
  3. Files here can be opened by anyone who has the web address, so keep private client plans elsewhere, or give them a name only you know.
Screenshot to add: the Meal plans screen with the sample flag

14Backups

  • Every night a copy of your content, orders and messages is written to a private folder on the server (the newest 14 are kept).
  • Overview → Download backup gives you the same file on your computer. Keep one somewhere safe now and then.

15If something looks wrong

  • Reload the page first.
  • If a change looks wrong on the site, Publish → History → Restore the previous version.
  • Contact your developer if the hosting check on Overview shows a red Fix item, or if signing in or publishing stops working.

Your first five minutes

Try these once, on your own — after that you know the panel:

  • Add a product with a photo, give it a price and some stock, then publish.
  • Mark something sold out.
  • Add an event with 8 places, then cancel it.
  • Change a package price.
  • Write and publish a Journal article.
  • Take an order through to Delivered.
  • Upload a meal plan and make it the sample.
  • Roll a publish back to the previous version.
You can't break it. If a test change ever looks wrong, Discard all unpublished changes puts everything back, or History → Restore returns an earlier version. Nothing is ever lost.

A month in Shanghai

Evidence and tradition were never enemies. One month at the Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, following curiosity.

In 2025 I travelled to China for a month to study Traditional Chinese Medicine at the Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, through the Study in China International Summer School.

I didn't go to swap one way of looking at health for another. I'm a dietitian, and I work from evidence. I went because I was curious — about a system of health that has shaped how millions of people eat, rest and look after themselves for centuries, and about what it might add to the way I already work.

What I went looking for

Nutrition science is very good at the measurable: grams, calories, blood markers. Traditional approaches tend to start somewhere else — with rhythm, balance, the season, the whole person. The more I work with women one-on-one, the more I see that both conversations matter.

What I brought home

Mostly, a wider lens. The month deepened my interest in holistic approaches to health, and it's part of why my consultations look at how you sleep, move, rest and live — not only at what's on your plate.

Evidence and tradition were never enemies. Curiosity is how you find where they meet.

Back home, SheOnTheRun kept growing, and DietOnTheRun grew through my work with women one-on-one — both a little richer for that month.

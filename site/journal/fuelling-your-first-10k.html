Fuelling Your First 10K, the Simple Version

By Fatima Mouzahem · 22 June 2026

Photo — runner on race morning, natural light

Every runner training for their first 10K eventually asks me some version of the same question: what am I supposed to eat before this?

The honest answer is that it matters less than running forums make it sound — and more than "just eat normally" accounts for. Here's the version I actually give clients.

The night before

Eat a normal dinner, slightly higher in carbohydrate than usual, at a normal time. This is not the moment for an enormous pasta bowl you have never eaten before. A 10K does not deplete your glycogen stores the way a marathon does, and an unfamiliar, heavy meal is far more likely to sit badly than to help.

If you are nervous and your appetite disappears, that is normal. Something small and familiar beats forcing down a big plate.

Race morning

Eat something familiar 60 to 90 minutes before the start: toast with honey, a banana, oats with a little fruit. Enough that you are not running on empty, not so much that it is still sitting there at kilometre three.

Hydrate normally in the days beforehand rather than drinking a litre on the start line. A glass of water with breakfast is enough for this distance.

During the run

For most people, a 10K needs no mid-run fuelling at all. If it is hot, take water at the stations. Gels and sports drinks earn their place at longer distances — using them here mostly risks an unhappy stomach for no benefit.

Afterwards

This is the part people skip. Within roughly half an hour to an hour, have a mix of carbohydrate and protein: a sandwich, a smoothie with yoghurt and fruit, or simply your next proper meal if the timing lines up. Recovery nutrition is what lets you train again later that week without feeling flat.

The part that actually matters

One race-day breakfast matters far less than what you ate on the ordinary Tuesday training runs in the eight weeks beforehand. Consistent fuelling across a training block is what gets you to a start line feeling strong. The breakfast is just the last, smallest step.

Race-day nutrition is the five per cent that gets all the attention. Training-week nutrition is the ninety-five per cent that decides how you actually run.
